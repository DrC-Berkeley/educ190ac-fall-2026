Questions to think with

Critical theory & Marx
How do work, money, and ownership shape what counts as preparation in your field?
For example, consider an unpaid placement, a required credential, or access to AI tools.

Labaree
Where do democratic equality, social efficiency, and social mobility pull your field’s programs in different directions?
Think about participation in public life, preparation for work, and individual advancement.

Hochschild
What can schooling change about the inequalities surrounding your field?
Consider the role of resources and opportunities beyond school.

Westheimer
What kind of citizen or participant is this education preparing someone to become?
Think about personal responsibility, collective participation, or changing the conditions behind a problem.

You chose either Hochschild or Westheimer. If your partner read the other author, take turns introducing one key idea in your own words and an example from your field that helps explain it. The listener can ask a question before you connect the two readings.

Learning with your hands

Each week, I’ll share an idea from the learning sciences for you to consider as you design your DeCal. This week, let’s start with embodied cognition: how our bodies help us think and learn. We often talk about thinking as though it happens entirely inside the brain, but movement, sensation, and interaction with our surroundings can shape how we work through an idea. Clark and Chalmers take this question further in their extended-mind proposal: can a tool we rely on, such as a notebook, become part of the thinking process itself?

As someone who regularly speaks with my hands, I found this especially interesting: our gestures can express an idea we haven’t yet put into words. Susan Goldin-Meadow’s research has helped establish how gesture can reveal emerging understanding and contribute to learning. The scholarship reaches beyond children learning math to spatial problem-solving, moral reasoning, and storytelling across different ages. Gesture is one part of a much broader body of research on how movement and thought work together.

As you talk tonight, play with using your hands to show a hierarchy, competing purposes, or a path into opportunity. Notice your partner’s gestures, too. Does a movement help you see a relationship that their words haven’t quite captured? Keep that possibility in mind when you design how others will work through an idea.
