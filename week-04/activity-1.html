EDUC 190AC · WEEK 4 · LEARNING HOUR

Reading an educational choice

One example. One lens. A design choice to carry forward.

Pairs or trios · 25 minutes in groups

STEP 1 · 5 MINUTES

Bring one example

Everyone gets a turn.

Open PW3, Step 1: “Look again.” Share a course requirement, internship expectation, course description, or perspective in a reading list. Explain what you noticed and what you think it means.

Choose one to explore.

Use your pre-work, a partner’s example, or the textbook passages on PDF pages 9–10. Keep the source open. Speak, type, or read your notes.

A starting sentence: “My source shows ____. I think that matters because ____.”

Need a starting point?

Use the textbook comparison or a partner’s example if your pre-work is unavailable. Fellows: use your EDUC 190AC observation and your own learning goal.

STEPS 2 + 3 · 12 MINUTES TOGETHER

Try one lens

Draw a lens or choose one below. Use a passage from the reading to connect it to your example.

Common sense

Ideas we inherit can come to feel obvious, even when they conflict.

Name one assumption your example takes for granted.

Gross, pp. 52–54, 60–61.

Hegemony & consent

Institutions and familiar ideas can help secure support for a social order, alongside coercion.

Identify what could make people accept this arrangement.

Gross, pp. 59–64. A possible explanation is not a proven cause.

Contradictory consciousness

An inherited explanation can sit uneasily with what people learn through living and working.

Look for evidence of a tension between a familiar belief and lived experience.

Gross, pp. 52–53, 60–61. Do not assume another person’s beliefs.

Whose knowledge counts?

Curricula give some knowledge and perspectives more authority than others.

Identify a perspective that is centered or missing.

Apple, Preface to the Second Edition, pp. vii–ix.

Hidden curriculum

The way education is organized can teach expectations about authority and participation.

Consider what one rule or routine might teach beyond the stated content.

Gross, pp. 66–67. Check effects rather than assuming them.

Material conditions

Time, money, work, and access shape what people can do.

Identify one condition that enables or limits participation.

Week 3 bridge; Gross, pp. 61–62.

If a lens does not fit, choose another. You only need one.

CONTINUE YOUR 12-MINUTE CONVERSATION

Check your interpretation

What supports it?

Point to a detail in your source.

What might you be overlooking?

Name a limit, another explanation, or a perspective you still need.

Save one sentence: “Our interpretation is ____, supported by ____. We are still unsure about ____.”

STEP 4 · 8 MINUTES

Return to your outcome

Each person returns to their own proposal, or drafts one from today’s conversation. Explain your decision to a partner and save these notes.

My outcome

Learners will be able to…

My reason: I would keep or revise it because…

Possible evidence

Learners could show this by…

My open question: I still need to consider…

Bring your notes into Project Hour. Fellows: apply this to the ability you want to develop in supporting learning.
